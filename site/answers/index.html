Answers

What people ask before their first banya. Answered directly.

  • Is a banya the same as a sauna?

    No. A sauna is Finnish, dry and silent — you sit in it. A banya is Russian, wet and alive — you are worked on.

  • Where does banya come from?

    From the Slavic village bathhouse — for most of its history the cleanest and hottest room a family had, and the one they used for everything that mattered.

  • Why beat someone with oak branches?

    It is not beating. The venik moves hot air onto the skin, and the master uses it to put heat exactly where he wants it, in the amount your body will take.

  • What do the heat and the cold actually do?

    Heat raises core temperature and loads the heart and circulation the way moderate exercise does. Cold reverses it within seconds. Here is what is known about that, and what is not.

  • Why real fire, and never an electric heater?

    Because the steam comes off a heavy mass of stone that has been heated for hours, and an electric element cannot hold heat the way a fired stove does.

  • Why do athletes use banya?

    For recovery between hard sessions, for sleep, and for a nervous system that will not come down on its own. With one caveat about timing that most places will not mention.

  • Why do you call it Russian?

    Because it is. The origin is the credential, and softening it into something vaguer would make the thing itself harder to understand.

  • Why is it silent, and why do you take phones?

    Because nothing works while someone in the room might be performing, and a phone in the room means someone might be.

  • What actually happens in a session?

    Four rounds of heat, venik and cold water over about two hours, led by a master, in silence.

  • How hot is it, and is it safe?

    The air holds 180–210°F, but you are never left alone in it, and every round ends before it needs to.

  • What do I bring, and what happens to my phone?

    Swimwear, a towel and nothing else. Phones are collected at boarding and returned at the end.

  • How do I book, and how do I pay?

    Send an application through this site. A master replies, confirms a date with you, and sends a payment link from the business — card, receipt, no cash in an envelope.
