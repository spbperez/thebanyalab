Why beat someone with oak branches?

It is not beating. The venik moves hot air onto the skin, and the master uses it to put heat exactly where he wants it, in the amount your body will take.

The air directly against your skin sits still and insulates you. That layer is the reason a person can be in a room at 200°F at all. The venik breaks it. Every sweep pushes hot, wet air onto the body, and heat that was hanging in the room goes into you instead.

That is the whole mechanism, and it is why the venik is a tool rather than a ritual prop. Held high, it takes heat from the ceiling, where the hottest air collects. Pressed flat against the back, it holds that heat against the skin. Lifted and fanned, it cools. A master spends the round deciding where the heat goes and how fast.

The leaves matter for ordinary reasons. Oak is thick and stiff, carries hot steam well and survives several sessions. Birch is softer and more aromatic, and easier on skin that has not done this before. Both are cut fresh in early summer and soaked before use — a dry venik crumbles and scratches.

The percussion is real, and light. It brings blood to the skin and gives the body a steady stream of sensation to attend to. That is a large part of why a round feels like something being done to you rather than something you are getting through.
