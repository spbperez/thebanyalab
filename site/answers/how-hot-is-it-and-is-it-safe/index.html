How hot is it, and is it safe?

The air holds 180–210°F, but you are never left alone in it, and every round ends before it needs to.

The heat is real and the humidity is high, which makes it feel far stronger than a dry sauna at the same temperature. That is the point — the contrast between hot and cold is the mechanism.

You are not in there by yourself. The master reads breath, skin and posture through the whole round and adjusts the steam and the pressure of the venik to what your body is actually taking. Rounds are short. You leave the heat before you want to, not after.

Heat and cold contrast puts load on the heart. If you are pregnant, have a heart condition or blood pressure that is being managed, or you are on medication that affects how you handle heat, ask your doctor first and tell the master before the session. We would rather have that conversation at the dock than in the parilka.
