Is a banya the same as a sauna?

No. A sauna is Finnish, dry and silent — you sit in it. A banya is Russian, wet and alive — you are worked on.

A Finnish sauna runs 175–200°F at low humidity, and you sit in it alone. A Russian banya runs 180–210°F at 50–60% humidity, and a master moves the steam across your body with a bundle of fresh oak branches called a venik.

The American gym sauna is a third thing again: 150–175°F, almost no humidity, and water on the stones is often banned outright for liability reasons. Most people in Hawaii have never experienced either real tradition.

The practical difference is that a banya is done to you, in rounds, over about two hours, with cold water between them. A sauna is something you sit in for fifteen minutes.
