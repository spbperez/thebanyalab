The two rituals

Both are banya. Heat, venik, cold, silence. What changes is the stage, not the essence.

The Lab Session

A sailing vessel in Ala Wai Harbor

The full ritual, held at the dock. Small group, four rounds, two hours.

The flow

  1. Arrival — phones surrendered, tea, silence.
  2. First round — gentle heat, the body adapts.
  3. Par — the master works the body with venik.
  4. Cold — plunge or cold water poured over you.
  5. Rest — on deck, in silence.
  6. Repeat — three to four rounds in total.
  7. Afterglow — tea, water, no conversation.

The Ocean Session

Under sail, open Pacific

The same ritual, taken out of the harbour. The cold plunge becomes the ocean.

The flow

  1. The vessel leaves the harbour — movement becomes part of the ritual.
  2. The same four rounds of heat and venik.
  3. The plunge is the open Pacific — deeper, saltier, colder than any tub.
  4. Between rounds — live sound: singing bowls, hang drum. Never speakers.
  5. Silence during par. Conversation only in the afterglow.
  6. Sunset or night. The light works with the ritual.

What it costs

  • $1,000–1,200 for the group — Private outing

By application. Members of the Lab first.

About 3 hours · 4 to 5 guests

The words we use

Banya
The ritual itself. Never sauna, never steam room, never bathhouse.
Par
The act of working the body with steam and venik. The verb and the craft.
Venik
A bundle of fresh oak or birch branches, used by the master.
Master
Never attendant, never therapist. The master leads the ritual.
Afterglow
The state afterwards. What people come back for.
